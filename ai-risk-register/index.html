AI Risk Register

A structured register for tracking risks across AI systems: how they're identified, scored, assigned an owner, mitigated and reviewed over time. Built to sit alongside AISIA, the AI System Impact Assessment tool, but usable on its own for any AI system inventory.

PurposeContinuous risk tracking, not a one-time assessment
ScopeAny AI system, model or vendor-supplied AI component
FormatMethodology below · downloadable Excel template

Why a separate register

An assessment is a snapshot. A register is a ledger.

An impact assessment such as AISIA answers one question at one point in time: should this system be deployed, and under what conditions? A risk register answers a different question, continuously: of everything we said we'd manage, what's still open, who owns it, and is it still accurate? Under ISO/IEC 42001:2023 clause 6.1.3 and the EU AI Act's Article 9 risk management system, organisations operating AI are expected to maintain exactly this: a living record, reviewed on a schedule, not a document filed away after go-live.

Risks land on this register from several sources: an AISIA assessment identifying a significant or critical impact, a DPIA finding, a post-deployment monitoring alert, an audit finding, an incident, or a new regulatory obligation. Wherever a risk originates, it follows the same lifecycle below.

Methodology · Step 1

Risk lifecycle

Five stages, repeated for every risk on the register. Nothing is closed until residual risk is documented and accepted by an owner.

01

Identify

Sourced from AISIA, DPIA, audits, monitoring alerts or incidents.

02

Assess

Rate likelihood and severity, 1–4 each. Score = likelihood × severity.

03

Treat

Choose avoid, mitigate, transfer or accept. Assign an owner and target date.

04

Monitor

Track status until mitigation is verified, not just marked complete.

05

Review

Re-score on a fixed schedule or when the system materially changes.

Methodology · Step 2

Scoring matrix

Likelihood and severity are each rated 1–4. The product gives a risk score from 1 to 16, grouped into three tiers, the same thresholds used in AISIA's impact rating, so a risk carries a consistent meaning whether it was raised during an assessment or added directly to the register.

Methodology · Step 3

Risk categories

Categories follow ISO/IEC 23894's AI risk sources and the EU AI Act's requirements, adapted into nine practical groupings a business analyst can apply without a data science background.

Bias & fairness

Discriminatory or disparate outcomes across protected groups.

Data quality & governance

Incomplete, unrepresentative or poorly lineage-tracked training data.

Privacy

Personal data exposure, re-identification, unlawful processing.

Security & robustness

Adversarial inputs, model drift, resilience to manipulation.

Transparency & explainability

Inability to explain a decision to the person affected by it.

Human oversight

Insufficient ability for a human to review, override or halt the system.

Accountability & governance

Unclear ownership, missing sign-off, no escalation path.

Third-party & vendor

Risk inherited from a vendor model, API or embedded AI component.

Legal & regulatory

Non-compliance with the EU AI Act, GDPR or sector-specific law.

Methodology · Step 4

Treatment options

Every risk on the register is assigned one treatment. "Mitigate" is the default for significant and critical risks; the other three are valid, deliberate choices, not fallbacks.

Avoid

Change scope, data or deployment so the risk no longer applies.

Mitigate

Reduce likelihood or severity through a specific, owned control.

Transfer

Shift exposure via contract, insurance or a vendor SLA.

Accept

Document residual risk and get sign-off from the system owner.

Example register

RR-EX-01 Health risk-prediction algorithm Bias & fairness Widely used commercial algorithm under-scored Black patients' care needs because it predicted cost rather than illness, reducing access to extra care for affected patients. 3 × 4 = 12 Critical Public case study Documented finding 2019
RR-EX-02 Epic Sepsis Model (ESM) Security & robustness Proprietary sepsis-prediction model built into hospital EHR software missed most sepsis cases across independent external validations, despite broad clinical deployment. 4 × 4 = 16 Critical Public case study Documented finding 2021
RR-EX-03 Zillow Offers / Zestimate Data quality & governance Home-pricing model trained on a stable market couldn't adapt to a rapid shift, causing systematic overpayment for properties and a >$500M write-down. 2 × 3 = 6 Significant Public case study Business unit discontinued 2021
RR-EX-04 Warehouse productivity system Human oversight Algorithmic pace-tracking set productivity targets and triggered automated warnings with minimal human review, contributing to elevated injury rates. 3 × 4 = 12 Critical Public case study Under regulatory review 2024
RR-EX-05 Customer Support Chatbot Transparency & explainability Users are not told they are interacting with an AI system before escalation to a human. 3 × 2 = 6 Significant Product Owner, CX Mitigated 2027
RR-EX-06 Third-Party Document OCR API Third-party & vendor Vendor has not disclosed retraining schedule or data retention terms for uploaded documents. 2 × 3 = 6 Significant Procurement / Legal In progress 2026
